About

Robustness for Systems That Interact

Interacting systems—gene regulatory networks, social webs, power grids, financial markets, robot fleets and, increasingly, populations of LLM agents that consume each other's outputs—rarely come with explicit dynamical laws, so they are modeled from data. Yet standard learning methods break down under distribution shift and high noise, and give unreliable predictions in rare regimes.

Interconnection makes this harder in three ways: feedback lets a model's decisions shift the data it is later evaluated on; propagation turns local perturbations into system-wide shifts; and the relevant "distribution" is over coupled, non-stationary, structured objects rather than i.i.d. samples.

How can we design learning methods that remain robust, reliable and generalizable when learning from, and acting on, interacting systems under distribution shift?

REALIS brings together communities that rarely meet—distributionally robust optimization, causality and invariance, statistical inference and uncertainty quantification, and control and reinforcement learning—to ask what transfers between their toolkits when the system is coupled and has feedback.

Application Areas

Multi-Agent LLMs

Agents act on each other's outputs, so one agent's drift becomes another's distribution shift

Power Networks

Learned controllers that stay safe on a coupled grid where local faults cascade

Genomics

Gene regulatory network inference for cancer drug response that transfers across cell lines and patients

Robotics

Robots and fleets that adapt to changing environments while exploring safely

Financial Networks

Systemic-risk forecasts that hold in rare stress regimes and under their own feedback

Dates & Format

Everything You Need to Know

Important Dates

  • Call for papers: December 18, 2026
  • Submission deadline: January 29, 2027 (AoE)
  • Notification: March 5, 2027
  • Camera-ready: April 9, 2027
  • Workshop: May 3, 2027

Submission Format

Regular papers up to 8 pages; short papers up to 4 pages for preliminary results, position papers and negative results.

Non-archival, double-blind, via OpenReview. Each paper receives at least two reviews.

Talks & Awards

All accepted papers are presented as posters. Four are selected for contributed talks, with priority to students and early-career researchers.

We award a best paper and a best student paper.

Inclusion

Mentoring tables pair first-time authors with senior researchers. We are seeking sponsorship for travel grants for participants from underrepresented groups and regions.

Virtual Access

Papers will be public on OpenReview, and talks streamed and posted with speaker consent. Authors unable to travel (e.g., visa issues) may present posters virtually.

Venue

The workshop is held on the day before the AISTATS 2027 main conference (May 4–6) in Montréal, Canada. Room details will follow once the conference schedule is out.

Open-Problems Breakout Tables

  • Multi-agent LLMs: how do we certify a pipeline whose agents shift each other's input distributions?
  • Power grids: which robustness guarantees survive the move from simulation to a coupled physical network?
  • Gene regulatory networks: when is an inferred network stable enough to support drug-response predictions?
  • Robotics: how should safe exploration trade off against adaptation when the environment changes?
  • Financial networks: can systemic-risk forecasts be made robust to the feedback they create?

Table notes feed a post-workshop report on open problems and candidate benchmarks.

Past Events

Where REALIS Comes From

Reliable ML 2026, the ETH-EPFL summer school Reliable Machine Learning: from LLMs to Cyber-Physical and Biological Systems (ETH Zürich, August 31 – September 3, 2026), brought together invited speakers from academia and industry for four days of lectures, poster sessions and hands-on sessions. REALIS 2027 is its first edition as a conference workshop.

Speakers included Daniel Kuhn, Andreas Krause, Jonas Peters, Christina Heinze-Deml, Claire Vernade, Michael Kirchhof, Jörn Jacobsen, Markus Wulfmeier, Soroosh Shafiee, Amine Bennouna, Clément Hongler, Emmanuel Abbé and Florian Tramèr.
